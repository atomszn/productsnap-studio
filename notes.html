Chapter 05 · how the thinking actually works

Before anything here was real, it was just a messy thought.

This is the part of the notebook where I show the work behind the work — what sparks an idea, the questions I argue with, what I try, and what actually ships. Not a blog. Not a changelog. Just how one product person thinks, learns, and builds.

↳ Four stations. Follow them top to bottom — or wander the board.

  1. 01
    Inputs

    Where it starts

    Ideas usually arrive messy. That's normal.

    • Macro signals and market shifts I notice moving across the things I read and watch.
    • AI building tools getting visibly better almost every week — what was hard last month is suddenly easy.
    • Podcasts, long conversations, books, and rabbit holes that make me rethink something I assumed was settled.
    • Real product friction in everyday tools — the small "why does it work this way?" moments.
    • Conversations with AI where the first answer isn't enough, so I keep pushing until the idea gets sharper.
    • Late-night and weekend building sessions where something feels off, and I have to simplify it.

    I write them down before they evaporate. Most go nowhere. A few don't.

  2. 02
    The messy middle

    The questions every idea has to survive

    If it can't answer these, it isn't ready.

    • What actually changed?
    • What assumption am I making?
    • Is this useful — or just interesting?
    • Can this be simpler?
    • Would someone genuinely care?
    Looked brilliant at midnight… needed coffee in the morning.
    AI liked this idea a little too much. I did not.
    Interesting, but not useful yet — parked.
    If I have to explain it three times, the design is probably wrong.

    A real one: the flashcard back. The honest question was "can someone actually learn from one card without scrolling or filler?" — which forced a no-internal-scroll, no-repeated-labels, one-complete-thought design.

  3. 03
    Experiments

    Things I actually tried

    Some shipped. Some taught me something. Same thing, really.

    • The flashcard idea, built for real — it became the ProductSnap App.
    • Can scattered signals become one better product question? — that question is Pulse.
    • Every screen started as a pencil drawing first — the ones worth keeping live in Sketches.
    • AI workflows — building the whole thing in plain English, and arguing with AI until it's right.
    still circling — not built yet

    An AI product coach inside ProductSnap — not just answers, but practice reps, product judgment, and feedback that feels personal.

    Agent-readable portfolios — making this site understandable not just to people, but to the AI agents that may evaluate work in the future.

  4. 04
    Shipped & learned

    Honest outcomes — not all wins

    Good product thinking is iteration you're willing to show.

    • Shipped The ProductSnap App went live — Build 9, on the App Store and Google Play.
    • Simplified Killed the watch-out box and the internal scrolling on the flashcard. One card, one complete thought.
    • Changed direction Moved from local build packaging to GitHub repos, so nothing got lost between sessions.
    • Parked Fully autonomous Pulse publishing. The system can detect when the story may need to change, but I parked auto-publishing until the trigger logic proves itself over real data.
    • Parked Notes-as-a-blog. I don't want this to become another content feed. If there's nothing real to say, it should stay quiet.
    • Still evolving Pulse — still tuning how scattered signals turn into one clear question.

    None of this is finished. That's the point.
